### Rule: Specify issue

When specifying the issue, make sure to include the tool name and the version being tested, as well as the associated behavior (issue).

- Wrong: "`Tool` is crashing as described."
- Right: "`Tool` `version` is `observed behavior that matches issue` instead of `expected behavior.`"


### Rule: Do not commit to timelines

Do not promise delivery within a specific timeframe or promise an outcome I cannot control. I can state the evidence or artifact I intend to produce.

- Wrong: "Should not be too dificult can be done soon."
- Right: "Will gather evidence and write up a repro-report along with plan of action."


### Rule: State that as a new software engineer it is my first attempts at open source contributions

When relevant, be transparent that this is one of my first open-source contributions. Do not exaggerate my familiarity with the repository or present myself as more experienced than I am. Do not act like people on stackoverflow.

- Wrong: "I am new to software engineering and may need help."
- Right: "This is one of my first attempts at open source contributions, thank you."


## Things I never post

<!-- A short list. Promises you cannot keep, tones you refuse,
shortcuts you know you reach for when tired. The skill quotes this
list back at you when a draft crosses it. -->

- Specific timelines or promises about when I will finish a fix.
- Claims that I fixed, reproduced, or understand something that my evidence does not support.
- Promises that a change will be merged or accepted.
- Overconfident language that makes me sound more experienced with the repository than I am.
- Unprofessional, dismissive, or argumentative responses to maintainers or other contributors.
- Generic AI-sounding filler, excessive praise, or unnecessary enthusiasm.
